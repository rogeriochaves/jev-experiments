Latency

Round trip is flat until the state gets big

One request at a time, five repeats per point, p50 shown. The state is a synthetic support transcript; the questions are yes/no Nouls. Input size barely moves latency until ~13k tokens, and 50 questions cost about the same as one. Whatever runs behind the endpoint is not paying per token the way a decoder does. About 100 ms of every number here is the Atlantic.

Concurrency

Throughput climbs to 512 in flight, then the limiter draws its line

Each burst sends a fixed batch at a given number of in-flight requests. A single request carries one ~1.9k-token conversation and three Nouls. A packed request carries ten conversations and thirty Nouls (twelve is the most that fit under the 32k state cap). Single requests never saw a 429 up to 470k tokens/s in a burst; packed requests started getting rate limited around 340k tokens/s. Latency is the price of queueing: p50 goes from 270 ms at 8 in flight to 2 s at 512.

Sustained

Sixty seconds at 256 in flight: a ten-second burst, then a steady 315k tokens/s

The first bucket runs at 480k tokens/s and 163 requests/s with zero 429s. From the second bucket on, the limiter holds the account at roughly 315k tokens/s and 108 requests/s, and every extra request comes back as a 429 that the client retries with backoff. That steady line is the number to plan around, and it is above the 250k tokens/s the docs promise. The tail after 60 s is the client draining retries.

Limits

What one call can hold

Question count is not free in latency once it gets large: 400 Nouls answered in 2.8 s, 1000 in 6.7 s, 2000 in 13.7 s, under load from the other benchmarks. The 255-option cap is a hard validation error, which is why the caveman vocabulary is exactly 255 entries.

Accuracy

Is the customer annoyed? Jev beats gpt-5-mini, until you pack the request

300 synthetic support conversations generated by gpt-5-mini with a known label: 75 each of explicit annoyance, subtle annoyance (curt replies, "as I said", asks for a human, no complaint words), neutral, and satisfied. One Noul per conversation, one 4-way Choice per conversation, the same Noul with eight conversations packed per request, and gpt-5-mini answering the same question as a baseline.

Packing eight conversations into one request saved 23% of the tokens and lost 11 points of accuracy: the answers for one conversation are leaking from its neighbours. Their docs warn about this ("large state full of irrelevant detail"); it shows up on a 25k-token request already. Raising the single-request threshold to 0.7 takes accuracy to 100% on this set. On the 4-way Choice, Jev separated explicit from subtle annoyance perfectly and confused neutral with satisfied 38 times, which reads as a fault of the generated labels more than of the judge: the "neutral" scripts often end with a thank you.

Toy 1

cavejev: a next-word loop over 255 caveman words

Jev does not generate. So the loop asks a Choice with the whole vocabulary as options, reads the probability of every word, and samples the next one with the same knobs a normal decoder has: temperature, top-k, top-p, a repetition penalty, frequency and presence penalties, no-repeat trigrams, and a ban on the same word twice in a row. Greedy decoding with none of that produces exactly the degeneration you would expect: "do light do light do light do light".

Why the hierarchical mode works: a peaked distribution

The flat 255-way Choice returns a bag of relevant words with the top candidate at 5 to 28%. Asking "which word class comes next" plus one Choice per class in the same request, then multiplying, gives a distribution with a clear winner at every step. Toy 2

codejev: JavaScript through a grammar, so it always compiles

Instead of predicting characters, the generator keeps a stack of pending grammar items and at every cursor asks Jev only about what is valid there: which statement kind, which expression kind, which operator, which in-scope variable, which builtin. Speculative fan-out resolves a whole node per request. Nothing it writes can fail to parse, which is the "push back before it writes something uncompilable" idea taken to the limit. Identifiers are caveman nouns. Sixteen tasks, six candidates each: candidate 0 greedy, the rest sampled at temperature 0.6.

Method and caveats

Read before quoting a number

Everything is Go, raw HTTP against POST /v1/systemone, one process on a laptop in Amsterdam. Latency includes about 100 ms of network. The client retries 429 and 5xx with exponential backoff and honours Retry-After.

Rate limits are dynamic. TypeSafe says so on the models page. The 315k tokens/s steady line and the 480k burst are what this key saw today; they can move.

Runs overlapped. The limits probe and part of codejev ran while other benchmarks were hammering the endpoint, which inflates some of their latencies (the 1000-Noul call at 6.7 s, the packed probe at 14 s). The latency chart ran alone.

The dataset is synthetic. gpt-5-mini wrote the conversations with the label in the prompt, so the labels are clean but the distribution is not production traffic. The neutral/satisfied confusion says as much about the generator as the judge.

Packing is a real trade. It cuts cost, and it also cuts accuracy, on this data by 11 points at eight per request. For a judging job, one trace per request with many questions is the right shape; the questions are nearly free.

Text generation stays a stunt. Sequential mode is 3 words/s and mostly noise; hierarchical mode answers real questions in short caveman sentences at 2.5 words/s and half a cent per reply; mask-predict is the fastest wall clock and by far the most expensive, because every slot repeats the 255-option list.

Code generation surprised me. Greedy solves 9 of 16 tasks, best-of-6 solves 12, at $0.055 for 96 programs. The factorial miss is my grammar offering only < as a loop bound. The array tasks fail on Math.max(tribe, ...): Jev knows which builtin, not that it needs a spread.
